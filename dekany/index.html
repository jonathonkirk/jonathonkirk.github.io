Dekany Explorer

Pick a tonic pitch and five odd numbers, and this builds the ten-note dekany scale (like the hexany) they generate (Erv Wilson's combination-product-set method — the same idea as a hexany, one size up: five generators instead of four, combined either two or three at a time). Click the keys to hear it — click several in quick succession to hear them stack into a chord.

Sine tones with a short attack and a slow decay, so overlapping clicks sustain into chords — not meant to imitate a piano, just to make the tuning audible. Tonic frequency is computed in 12-tone equal temperament with A4 = 440 Hz; the dekany notes themselves are just intonation and will generally fall between the piano's keys. Each product is octave-reduced directly against that tonic (same method as the hexany explorer) — some other references build a dekany by dividing every product by the smallest one first, which picks a different note as the root and gives different-looking numbers for the same idea.
